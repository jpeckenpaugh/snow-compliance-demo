Key point A policy-filtered query returns zero rows, not an error. Verification therefore records row counts alongside the query ID, and confirms the policy is attached via INFORMATION_SCHEMA.POLICY_REFERENCES.
Evidence

Where the proof comes from

  • SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.QUERY_HISTORY — who ran what, as which role, and when (note: ~45 min latency).
  • SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.ACCESS_HISTORY — which columns/tables were touched.
  • INFORMATION_SCHEMA.POLICY_REFERENCES — which policy is attached to which column/table.
  • SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE.ROW_ACCESS_POLICIES — policy definitions in effect.
SELECT query_id, user_name, role_name, start_time, query_text
FROM snowflake.account_usage.query_history
WHERE role_name IN ('ATTORNEY','AUDITOR')
  AND query_text ILIKE '%fact_clause%'
ORDER BY start_time DESC;

Deliverable

Unresolved high-risk clauses by authorized matter

The closing artifact of the demo: for the signed-in role, list high-risk clauses on the matters they are authorized to see, with the contract, clause category, and score.

SELECT matter_id, matter_name, contract_type, clause_category,
       risk_score, risk_band
FROM marts.mart_matter_risk
WHERE risk_band = 'High'
ORDER BY risk_score DESC;

Run as ATTORNEY_A and ATTORNEY_B, the same statement yields two different, correctly scoped reports — the single clearest demonstration of the wall.

Ongoing

Continuous governance

  • Alerts on policy changes (POLICY_REFERENCES diffs) and on unexpected role grants.
  • A weekly access-review report listing authorizations expiring within 30 days.
  • Row-access tests re-run on a schedule so regressions are caught, not hoped for.
