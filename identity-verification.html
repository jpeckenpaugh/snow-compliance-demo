Biometric data — approval gate This is a proposal, not a working capture feature. Facial biometrics are sensitive personal information and are regulated in several jurisdictions. Do not enable real capture, store real employee photos, or pilot with real people until legal review, a DPIA, written consent, retention approval, and vendor due diligence are complete. This page and the full guide are design material, not legal advice.
Recommendation

Add verification as a step-up authenticator

A face is not a secret. A printed photo or replayed video can be presented to a camera, so webcam verification must sit after password + MFA and raise an authentication assurance level. It must never become the only factor protecting privileged roles.

DecisionRecommendationReason
Matching mode1:1 self-verification1:N identification creates much larger privacy, bias, and false-match exposure.
Enrollment3–5 consented framesOne frame cannot estimate within-person variance or calibrate a threshold.
Raw photo retention90 days defaultEnough for audit; not an indefinite biometric archive. Legal hold overrides deletion.
EmbeddingsEmployment + audit tailVectors are still biometric data; delete on separation or withdrawal.
ThresholdsCalibrated, never guessedPublished numbers from other deployments do not transfer to our cameras and population.
FallbackAssisted, never silentOutages and accommodations must not become an authentication bypass.
User experience

Enrollment and daily sign-in

1. Consent and enroll

After password + MFA, explain purpose, retention, viewers, and the voluntary alternative. Capture 3–5 frames with a random challenge and quality/liveness gates. Store the template and centroid version.

2. Sign in

Password + MFA produce a short-lived pre-auth session. The camera step captures a fresh frame with a server nonce, then runs quality → liveness → embedding → 1:1 comparison.

3. Issue assurance

On accept, the token carries verification_id, assurance_level, model_id, centroid_version, and verified_at. Snowflake maps that to a role.

4. Step up when it matters

Opening a High-confidentiality matter or changing policy requires a fresh verification (for example, within 5 minutes) with its own audit event.

Failure behavior is part of the design Poor lighting gets guidance; liveness failure gets one retry then a deny and an alert; a review-band score never auto-accepts; service outages fail closed for privileged roles and route to an assisted path with two-person approval.

The face model runs in a container service or vendor API, not as a Snowflake-native function. Only the resulting vector and decision metadata are written to Snowflake. Snowflake Cortex text embeddings are not a face-embedding substitute.

AI / ML

Embeddings and a centroid that does not drift away

Use an ArcFace/InsightFace-family model that outputs an L2-normalized 512-dimensional vector, or a vendor verification API with a calibrated score. Record the exact model, preprocessing, and threshold versions. Pin artifact hashes and treat a model change as a new template generation.

c_0 = normalize(mean(e_1 ... e_n))          # enrollment centroid
d_i = 1 - cosine(e_i, c_0)                 # intra-user distance
p50, p95 = percentiles(d_i)
alpha_eff = max(0.05, 1 / (sample_count + 1))
c_new = normalize((1 - alpha_eff) * c_old + alpha_eff * e_accepted)

Update only on an accept with a margin above threshold, good liveness and quality, no legal hold, and no outlier flag. Cap updates at three per day and keep a version history with rollback.

Centroid riskGuard
One bad frame pulls the templateOutlier gate at the user's p99 + margin; reject the update
Repeated spoofs shift identityLiveness gate, daily cap, spoof alerts, quarantine after anomalies
Camera or model driftCohort monitoring, per-user drift alerts, recompute on model upgrade
Attacker re-enrolls the accountMFA + manager/compliance approval + cooling-off period
Silent model changeNew model_id; rebuild centroids from active templates; retain old vectors for audit
Corrupt centroidVersion history, checksum, two-person review for forced updates, rollback
Verification

Three bands, with liveness overriding the score

BandSimilarityOutcomeUser experience
Accept≥ 0.68acceptSign in with the required assurance
Review0.58–0.679reviewAssisted verification; never auto-accept
Deny< 0.58denyNeutral failure, retry policy, security log

Illustrative starting points for an ArcFace-family model. Calibrate on a consented pilot and approve the FAR/FRR risk appetite before production. Liveness or quality failure is always a deny, even if the similarity is high.

RBAC

Assurance levels map to real Snowflake roles

RoleRequired assuranceStep-upPhoto / embedding access
PARALEGALAAL2High mattersNo
ATTORNEYAAL2High mattersNo
AUDITORAAL2NeverBreak-glass only
COMPLIANCE_ADMINAAL3Policy changesYes, with reason + log
DEMO_ADMINAAL3 + managed deviceAlwaysEmergency only
  • Use external OAuth or governed token exchange so the app never holds a Snowflake password.
  • Never trust a client-supplied assurance_level; read it from the signed token or server-side verification record.
  • Bind the token to the verification event and expire it quickly; re-check freshness for step-up actions.
  • Keep matter confidentiality in row access policies. Add assurance through roles/entitlements, not unverified session text.
  • Break-glass is time-bound, two-person approved, reason-coded, and fully audited — not a silent fallback.
Audit & retention

Provable decisions, deletable photos

ArtifactDefault retentionDeletion trigger
Raw login photo90 daysConsent withdrawal, erasure request, successful appeal
Enrollment photosEmployment + 90 daysSeparation + audit tail, withdrawal
Template / embeddingEmployment + audit tailSeparation, withdrawal, model retirement
Login metadata2 yearsInvestigation hold
Audit hash chain7 yearsLegal requirement
Photo access log7 yearsLegal requirement

Every attempt records the outcome, scores, model/centroid version, photo hash, request ID, IP/user agent, and resulting assurance. Every human view of a raw photo writes a photo_access_log row with a reason and approver. Photos use Object Lock and lifecycle deletion; legal hold blocks removal. The hash chain is periodically anchored in immutable storage, and Snowflake ACCESS_HISTORY provides independent evidence.

Implementation

What to build, in order

  1. Legal & design gate

    DPIA, consent text, retention schedule, vendor selection, works council review, threat model. No real captures.

  2. Sandbox

    Integrate the model behind the reference interface; apply the IDENTITY DDL; calibrate thresholds; verify the audit chain and deletion workflow on synthetic data.

  3. 20-person pilot

    Both camera types, accessibility testing, assisted path, FAR/FRR measurement, bias review. Do not scale on a single happy path.

  4. Privileged roles

    Compliance admin, auditors, and High-matter attorneys. Exercise step-up, break-glass, and rollback drills.

  5. General rollout

    Training, support runbook, quarterly access review, retention-job monitoring, board reporting.

Reference logic: poc/face_verification.py implements the deterministic parts — normalization, cosine matching, centroid guards, quality/liveness gates, three-band decisions, and a hash chain. It uses a test-only hashing embedder; replace that with the approved model. Run python3 -m unittest poc/test_face_verification.py -v.

Decisions requested

What the board needs to settle

  1. Mandatory, voluntary, or role-based participation — and the approved alternative for people who decline or cannot use a camera.
  2. Self-hosted model vs. vendor API; if vendor, which regions and retention terms.
  3. Raw-photo retention period and who may approve legal hold.
  4. Which roles require AAL2 vs. AAL3, and which matters require fresh step-up.
  5. FAR/FRR risk appetite and who signs off on threshold calibration.
  6. Re-enrollment approval path and the two break-glass approvers.
